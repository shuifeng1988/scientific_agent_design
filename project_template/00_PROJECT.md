# Project purpose and scientific rationale

_Drafting guide, not an approved project. Write in the user's language. Complete the choice-assisted direction discussion before using this template; expand it after actual deep research and obtain separate formal design approval. Replace prompts with sourced prose, not invented facts._

## 1. Research problem and intended decision

Explain the specific scientific/practical uncertainty, affected population or application, context, and decision the study will inform. Define terms and the unit of analysis. Distinguish the researcher's original hypothesis from established evidence.

## 2. Existing evidence and unresolved gaps

Synthesize foundational and recent primary studies, including the closest work and contrary/null findings. Cite claims and link the detailed appraisal. State what is known, disputed, unmeasured, or not identifiable with the available evidence. Do not replace this argument with a bibliography.

## 3. Research significance and contribution

Explain the applicable scientific, practical and/or methodological value, who could use the answer, and why the work is timely and worth its cost. Compare the proposed contribution with the closest studies. Label replication, robustness testing, extension or new discovery honestly.

Explain what a null/negative finding would teach and what would make the project uninformative. Do not assert novelty from a failed search or promise that a preferred hypothesis will be supported.

## 5. Evidence strategy and feasibility

### Where does the central evidence come from?

Answer in plain language: which actual studies/data/measurements or derivations are decisive, why they distinguish alternatives, and whether they are already verified or only planned. Separate prior findings, our planned/actual analysis, corroboration and counterevidence. Skill/Agent sources are methodological acknowledgements, not scientific evidence.

State what would weaken the central claim, whether validation reuses discovery samples, and what changes if its strongest source or required measurement is unavailable. Do not rank evidence by prestige, paper count or dataset size alone.

Explain how the planned comparisons, controls and independent evidence distinguish the hypotheses. Reference detailed steps in 01_PLAN.md and catalogs in 02_SOFTWARE.md / 03_DATA.md.

Record data and measurement adequacy, independent replication, access/license/ethics, available methods/resources, material risks, alternatives and stopping criteria. Mark unverified availability or resource estimates as such. Unknown quantities require a readiness task, not a fabricated number.

## 6. Deliverables and interpretation limits

Map scientific questions to human-readable reports and machine-readable artifacts. Each registered analysis delivers methods, provenance, tables, figures, conclusions, limitations and aligned Markdown/Word reports under its result path. Register any final synthesis as a planned task too; do not invent an unlinked final-report location.

State who the deliverables serve, acceptable uncertainty, limits of generalization and what the available study cannot establish.

## 7. Decisions, evidence and version

Link the direction-confirmation record, final appraisal/search/method evidence, substantive design review and later formal approval. Record version/date, outstanding issues and material changes. Current root documents should be readable without browsing every raw search file. Frozen scope changes require reviewed amendments, not silent replacement.
